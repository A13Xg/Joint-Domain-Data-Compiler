Joint Domain Data Compiler — User Guide

A single-screen engineering workbench for TSPI (trajectory and flight-data) logs. Import seven formats, normalise them into one internal dataset, then inspect, transform, compare, and re-export them. Built for precision and auditability rather than dashboards.

Getting started

Everything happens on one screen. Load a file, and the tabs across the top become the different ways of looking at the same dataset — they are views, not a wizard, so you can move between them freely and your selection follows you.

  1. Press + Load data (or drag files onto the Import tab).
  2. A CSV takes you to CSV Mapping to confirm which columns are latitude, longitude, altitude, and time. Every other format loads straight through.
  3. Work through the tabs: Overview for a health scan, Map/Charts/3D to look at it, Transform to fix it, Export to write it back out.
Nothing leaves your machine. Parsing, analysis, and export all run locally in the app. The only network requests are optional map basemap tiles, and you can switch the Map tab to an offline grid to stop even those.

Importing data

Seven input formats, all normalised into one internal shape: degrees, metres, and epoch milliseconds. Conversion happens once, at import.

FormatExtensionsNotes
CSV / TSV.csv .tsv .txtAny column layout; you map the columns yourself on the next tab.
GPX.gpxTrack points, including extension channels.
GeoJSON.geojson .jsonLineString and Point features.
KML / KMZ.kml .kmzPlacemarks and Google gx:Track.
NMEA 0183.nmea .gps .logGGA, GLL, RMC, VTG, ZDA, GSA, and GSV sentences.
GPB.gpb .binThe app's own compact binary format.
EAG TSPI.txtNATO range instrumentation, including IRIG and range-time encodings.
Rejected records are counted, not hidden. If a parser refuses rows — missing coordinates, a bad checksum, an unparseable timestamp — the counts appear under Dropped at import on the Overview tab, and the reasons appear as warnings. A file that loaded with fewer points than you expected will say why.

CSV mapping

A CSV has no agreed schema, so this tab is where you say what its columns mean. It guesses first, and shows you how confident it is.

ControlWhat it does
Latitude / LongitudeRequired. The Build button stays disabled until both are set. A confidence percentage next to each shows how sure the detector was.
Swap hintIf the values look transposed — latitudes outside ±90, say — a banner offers to swap them rather than silently importing a track on the wrong side of the world.
Elevation + unitOptional. Pick the source unit; it is converted to metres on import.
Timestamp + formatOptional but strongly recommended: speed, resampling, gap filling, comparison, and most of Track Health all need time.
Header interpretationOverride when the first row is data rather than headers, or when data starts several rows down.
Build dataset from full CSVStreams the whole file with a progress bar you can cancel. The preview above is a sample; this reads everything.
Importing without a timestamp is allowed, and warned about. You will get a dataset, but any operation that needs time will be unavailable or will fall back to a simpler behaviour. The warning says so at import rather than leaving you to discover it.

Overview & Track Health

The first thing to look at after loading. Track Health scores the track out of 100 across seven checks; below it, the metrics and statistics describe what actually came in.

CheckWeightWhat it asks
Schema / ParseGateIs the dataset structurally sound and are the coordinates valid? A failure here blocks the rest.
Altitude Profile30Does this look like a complete flight — a climb through 1000 ft, sustained time above it, and a descent back below before the track ends?
Speed Envelope10Does derived speed stay between 10 kt and Mach 2 while under way? The parked portion at the start of a file is ignored.
Time Order & Span20Are timestamps non-decreasing, and is the total span plausible?
Outliers20Do more than 5% of samples break the local trend? Detection is turn-aware, so a sustained legitimate turn is not read as noise.
Stagnant20Is there a prolonged stationary segment in the middle of movement?
GPS Fix QualityInformationalReports degraded HDOP or low satellite count where the source carries them. Never affects the score.

Every flagged sample becomes a chip you can click to jump straight to that point on the Map or Charts tab. Where a check can offer a fix, it does — see Repairing a track.

Map

The track in plan view, with every other loaded track available as context.

ControlWhat it does

The map draws a budget, not the whole track. Above roughly 4,000 points (adjustable in Settings) the display is downsampled deterministically. The readout tells you how many of how many are drawn. Statistics and export always use every point.

Points

One sample at a time, in full, with its neighbours drawn around it. This is where you go when a single point looks wrong.

ElementWhat it shows
Quality stripThe whole track as a colour bar by severity. Click anywhere in it to jump to that region.
Neighbourhood planA local, equal-aspect plot of the surrounding samples with a scale bar — the view that makes a position spike obvious.
Point fieldsLatitude and longitude in both decimal and degrees-minutes-seconds, elevation, time in ISO and epoch milliseconds, and source provenance.

Compare

Two tracks, time-aligned, reduced to relative geometry: how far apart, in what direction, closing or opening.

ControlWhat it does
reference / targetWhich track is the origin of the relative frame, and which is measured against it.
tolerance (ms)How far apart two samples may be in time and still be paired. Widen it when the two sources log at different rates.
target offset (ms)Shift the target in time before aligning, to correct a known clock offset.
interpolate target positionsInstead of pairing with the nearest real sample, interpolate the target's position at each reference timestamp. More precise ranges, at the cost of the clock-drift estimate, which is not meaningful in this mode and is shown as unavailable rather than as zero.
Export comparison CSVWrites every aligned sample, not the first 250 the table displays.
Incompatible metadata blocks the comparison. If the two tracks declare different altitude or time references, the app refuses to compare them and says which fields disagree — rather than differencing two numbers that do not mean the same thing.

3D

The trajectory in space, with playback. Useful for reading vertical structure that a plan view flattens away.

ControlWhat it does

projectionPerspective for a natural view, orthographic for measuring shape without foreshortening.
ground grid / vertical curtainA reference plane, and dropped lines from the track to it, to make altitude legible.

Transform

Every operation that changes the data. All of them are undoable, all of them are recorded, and all of them preserve provenance.

OperationWhat it does
Sort by timeReorders samples into ascending time.
Swap lat/lonFixes a transposed import.
Drop invalidRemoves samples with unusable coordinates.
De-jitter timestampsCleans up equal or backward timestamps by nudging, dropping, or averaging them.
Clip to time windowKeeps only samples inside a start/end window, with an explicit policy for untimed points.
Drop outliersFinds samples breaking the local trend and, by default, refits them from their neighbours rather than deleting them. See below.
Elevation filterMedian, exponential-moving-average, or Hampel smoothing of the altitude channel only.
SmoothWindowed smoothing of position, elevation, or both.
Reduce pointsDeduplicate, decimate by a factor, or simplify to a distance tolerance.
Round precisionTrims stored decimal places on coordinates, elevation, and channels.
Resample to fixed rateRe-times the track onto a regular rate, optionally skipping large gaps instead of inventing across them.
Resample by distanceRe-samples at a fixed spacing along the path.
Fill gapsSynthesises samples across time gaps above a threshold, gated by a motion profile so the fit stays physically plausible.
Derive kinematicsComputes distance, speed, heading, turn rate, and acceleration channels from position and time.
Shift time / Offset elevationApplies a constant offset to every sample.

Motion profiles

Drop outliers and Fill gaps both take a motion profile — Aircraft, Ground vehicle, Marine, or None — which sets ceilings on speed, vertical speed, and turn rate. Any reconstructed sample that would violate those ceilings is rejected rather than written. Aircraft is the default; the default is configurable in Settings.

Repairing a track

When Track Health fails the outlier check, it offers the fix directly, using the same detector settings it graded against — so what is reported and what is repaired cannot disagree.

  1. Press Repair flagged points. The repair is computed but not applied.
  2. Read the fact list: how many points before and after, how many moved, and the largest position and elevation shift.
  3. Revert discards it and changes nothing. Accept applies it as a single undoable operation.
Repaired points are refitted, not deleted — and they say so. Flagged samples are re-derived from their surviving neighbours through the same plausibility-gated engine Fill gaps uses, so the track keeps its point count. Each one is marked interpolated in its provenance, counted on the Overview tab, and disclosed in the HTML report. A synthesised sample is never presented as an observed one.
A track with any untimed sample falls back to deletion. Reconstruction fits against time, so it cannot run without it. The app says so on the control rather than failing when you press it.

Fusion

Combine several sources covering the same event into one track, with a record of which source won at each moment.

Select at least two sources, give each a priority, set the time tolerance that decides which samples describe the same instant, and run Auto-Combine. The result is a new dataset — sources are never modified. The decision timeline records every choice, and manual overrides let you force a particular source over a chosen interval.

Fusion will not invent a common reference frame. Sources must declare the same coordinate system and have valid spatial coverage, or the run is blocked with the reason. Reproject explicitly first.

Export

Write the dataset back out. The preview pane shows what is actually about to be written before you commit to it.

FormatCarries
GPXCoordinates, elevation, time, and optionally extension channels. Options for sort-by-time, byte-order mark, and coordinate precision.
CSVA flat table of every field and channel.
GeoJSONStandard feature geometry.
KMLFor Google Earth and similar viewers.
EAG TSPINATO range format, with platform type, exercise, and mission identifiers.
GPBThe app's compact binary format — the most faithful for coordinates and numeric channels.
Exporting notional points requires acknowledgement. If a dataset contains synthesised samples — from Fill gaps, resampling, or a repair — export is gated behind an explicit checkbox. It exists so derived data is never handed on as observed data by accident.
Every format is lossy in its own way, and the warnings say how. GPB is numeric-only: it drops names, descriptions, and provenance including quality flags, and coerces non-numeric channels to zero. GPX and EAG have their own limits. Read the export warnings before treating a round trip as faithful.

The HTML analysis report

Nine evidence sections can be included or left out: source metadata, import warnings, quality events, bookmarks, operation history, cross-dataset comparison, fusion decisions, data quality, and the map overlay inventory. Comparison, fusion, and overlays are off by default; the rest are on. Remember these settings for this project saves your choice with the project — it is unchecked by default and nothing is persisted unless you tick it.

Reports are always in metres and m/s. The display-unit preference changes what you see on screen; it deliberately does not change the report, exports, or stored data, so a report means the same thing to whoever receives it.

Worked examples

Importing and mapping a CSV

  1. + Load data, choose the file. The CSV Mapping tab opens with its guesses filled in.
  2. Confirm latitude and longitude. Check the confidence figures, and take the swap hint seriously if it appears.
  3. Set the timestamp column and its format. Without it, speed, resampling, gap filling, and most of Track Health are unavailable.
  4. Set the elevation column and its source unit.
  5. Build dataset from full CSV, then go to Overview and read the health scan before doing anything else.

Comparing two tracks

  1. Load both. The Compare tab enables once there are two datasets.
  2. Pick reference and target. The reference defines the origin of the relative frame.
  3. If nothing aligns, raise the tolerance — the two sources probably log at different rates.
  4. If they are offset by a known clock error, put it in target offset rather than editing timestamps.
  5. Read the summary cards, then Export comparison CSV for every aligned sample rather than the 250 shown.

Repairing outliers

  1. Overview → look at the Outliers check. If it failed, the flagged samples are listed as chips.
  2. Click a chip to inspect that sample on the Map or Charts tab, and confirm it is genuinely bad rather than a real manoeuvre.
  3. Repair flagged points, read the preview, then Accept or Revert.
  4. Re-scan. The score should improve and the point count should be unchanged.
  5. If you disagree with the result, undo it — or use Transform → Drop outliers to run the same operation with your own thresholds and motion profile.

Exporting a dataset

  1. Export, choose the format card. Read the warnings listed for that format.
  2. Set the output name and any format options.
  3. Check the live preview — it is the real output, not a mock-up.
  4. If the dataset contains synthesised samples, acknowledge the notional-data gate.
  5. Export. For a record of how the data got to this state, also export the HTML report from the Project tab.

How your data is treated

The rules the app holds itself to. They explain most of its more cautious behaviour.

RuleWhat it means for you
Never fabricate dataDownsampling is display-only; export always writes the full dataset. Any synthesised sample is flagged in provenance, counted on Overview, and disclosed in reports. If a value cannot be honestly derived, it is dropped with a warning rather than guessed.
Transforms are pureAn operation produces a new dataset and preserves provenance and quality flags. Nothing is edited in place behind your back, which is why undo can always take you back.
Coordinate maths is antimeridian-safeTracks crossing 180° longitude interpolate the short way round, including angular channels like heading.
Untrusted input is validated at the boundaryOperation parameters and restored project files are checked before use, and malformed data is rejected loudly rather than half-applied.
One canonical unit systemDegrees, metres, and epoch milliseconds internally. Conversion happens once at import, and the display-unit preference never changes what is stored.

Troubleshooting

A tab is greyed out

It needs data it does not have. Most tabs need an active dataset; Compare and Fusion need two; Project needs at least one. CSV Mapping only appears while a CSV is waiting to be built.

A button is disabled

Usually a missing prerequisite rather than a fault. Build dataset needs latitude and longitude mapped. Reset to defaults is disabled when nothing differs from the defaults. Reconstruction options are disabled on a track with untimed samples, with a tooltip saying so.

The map is blank or the basemap will not load

Basemap tiles need a network connection. A banner appears when they fail, offering to switch to the offline grid — your track data is fully usable either way.

Fewer points loaded than the file contains

Check Dropped at import on the Overview tab and the import warnings. Records with unusable coordinates, bad checksums, or unparseable timestamps are counted and reported rather than silently included.

The track looks fine but Track Health scores it badly

Read the individual checks — the score is their weighted sum. The altitude profile check expects a complete takeoff-to-landing flight, so a mid-flight excerpt or a ground vehicle will legitimately fail it. A low score means "this is not the shape I expected", not "this data is corrupt".

Every point is flagged as an outlier

Check the motion profile. Detection is turn-aware, but a profile whose ceilings are far below the vehicle's real performance will flag normal manoeuvring. Try a profile that matches the platform, or None to disable the physical constraints.

Speeds or altitudes are in the wrong units

Settings → Display units switches every readout between metric and aviation/marine. If the values themselves are wrong rather than the units, check the elevation unit you chose at CSV mapping.

I need to get back to the original data

Undo steps back one operation at a time. Transform → Restore original returns the dataset exactly as imported, however many operations have been applied.

Something crashed or behaved oddly

Project → Export diagnostic bundle collects the logs and environment details, with a note you write yourself. It contains no point data.
